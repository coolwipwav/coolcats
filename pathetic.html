“I Stand By It”: Children of Emenike Refuse to Back Down After Calling Opponents “Pathetic”

A perfect 3–0 Matchweek 5 was followed by controversy — and when given the opportunity to clarify his remarks, Isaiah only doubled down.

The Lonestar Times Matchweek 5 Children of Emenike
Children of Emenike // Matchweek 5
Club Children of Emenike Record 3–0 Matchweek

Children of Emenike entered Matchweek 5 looking for victories.

They left with three of them — and perhaps a few new enemies along the way.

Following a perfect 3–0 weekend, Children of Emenike owner Isaiah caused a stir around the H-E-B Lonestar League after describing the opposition his club had faced as “pathetic.”

With criticism beginning to circulate around the league, Isaiah was given an opportunity to clarify his comments.

He did.

Just not in the way his critics might have hoped.

“I Stand By It.”

In a statement provided following the controversy, Isaiah first praised his own squad for completing the undefeated weekend.

“First and foremost, credit to the boys. Going 3–0 in a single weekend isn’t easy, and they handled their business exactly the way I expected them to.”

Then he addressed the word that had gotten everyone talking.

“I Stand
By It.”

Isaiah // Children of Emenike
“As for my comments after the matches, I’ve heard there’s been some concern over my use of the word ‘pathetic.’ I’d like to clear that up. I stand by it.”

There would be no apology.

No clarification that the comment had been taken out of context.

No attempt to cool tensions with the clubs Children of Emenike had just beaten.

Instead, Isaiah doubled down.

“We came into Matchweek 5 prepared, disciplined, and hungry. Our opponents had the same opportunity to do so. What happened on the pitch speaks for itself.”

And, unfortunately for his critics, Brian Brobbey made that argument considerably easier to make.

Brobbey Runs Riot

At the center of Children of Emenike’s dominant weekend was a staggering performance from Brian Brobbey, who erupted for a hat-trick and more than 42 fantasy points.

It was the kind of individual performance capable of swinging an entire matchup — and it provided the exclamation point on Children of Emenike’s perfect weekend.

While Isaiah’s comments have understandably dominated the headlines, Brobbey’s performance cannot be overlooked. When his owner began talking afterward, he did so having just watched one of his players deliver one of the league’s most explosive individual performances.

Three goals. More than 42 points.

Brobbey gave Children of Emenike plenty of ammunition.

Isaiah was more than happy to use it.

A Message Directly To Wooburt City

Isaiah then singled out Wooburt City, acknowledging that the Cool Cats had apparently taken particular offense to his remarks.

His response was hardly conciliatory.

“I understand Wooburt City may have taken particular offense. They’re entitled to their feelings. We’ll concern ourselves with results.”

That sentence may ultimately prove more inflammatory than the original insult.

Rather than engage with Wooburt City's criticism, Children of Emenike effectively dismissed it as hurt feelings from a defeated opponent.

Then came the closing line:

“Three Matches.
Three Wins.
We Move.”

Children of Emenike

A Target Has Been Placed

There is, admittedly, one problem with criticizing Isaiah at the moment:

He won.

Three matches.

Three victories.

A 42-plus-point hat-trick from Brian Brobbey.

And when challenged publicly afterward, he showed absolutely no interest in retreating from what he said.

That makes Children of Emenike one of the league’s most fascinating clubs going forward.

Confidence is easy when you're winning.

Maintaining it when the league begins circling your name on the calendar is considerably harder.

Because Isaiah has now ensured that future meetings with Children of Emenike will carry something extra.

For Wooburt City especially, the next matchup won't simply be another fixture.

They have been beaten.

They have been called pathetic.

And when they objected, they were told they were “entitled to their feelings.”

Those are the kinds of words that tend to survive long after a Matchweek ends.

For now, though, Children of Emenike hold the only response that really matters.
