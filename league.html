“I Stand By It”: Children of Emenike Refuse to Back Down After Calling Opponents “Pathetic”

The Lonestar Times

Children of Emenike entered Matchweek 5 looking for victories. They left with three of them — and perhaps a few new enemies along the way.

Following a perfect 3–0 weekend, Children of Emenike owner Isaiah caused a stir around the H-E-B Lonestar League after describing the opposition his club had faced as “pathetic.”

“I Stand By It.”

“First and foremost, credit to the boys. Going 3–0 in a single weekend isn’t easy, and they handled their business exactly the way I expected them to.”

Then he addressed the word that had gotten everyone talking.

“As for my comments after the matches, I’ve heard there’s been some concern over my use of the word ‘pathetic.’ I’d like to clear that up. I stand by it.”

There would be no apology. No clarification that the comment had been taken out of context. No attempt to cool tensions with the clubs Children of Emenike had just beaten.

“We came into Matchweek 5 prepared, disciplined, and hungry. Our opponents had the same opportunity to do so. What happened on the pitch speaks for itself.”

Brobbey Runs Riot

At the center of Children of Emenike’s dominant weekend was a staggering performance from Brian Brobbey, who erupted for a hat-trick and more than 42 fantasy points.

Three goals. More than 42 points. Brobbey gave Children of Emenike plenty of ammunition. Isaiah was more than happy to use it.

A Message Directly To Wooburt City

“I understand Wooburt City may have taken particular offense. They’re entitled to their feelings. We’ll concern ourselves with results.”

Then came the closing line:

“Three matches. Three wins. We move.”

There is, admittedly, one problem with criticizing Isaiah at the moment: he won. Confidence is easy when you're winning. Maintaining it when the league begins circling your name on the calendar is considerably harder.

For now, Children of Emenike hold the only response that really matters. Three matches. Three wins. And absolutely no apology.

Space Cadets In Revolt: Dowman Release Sparks Extraordinary Attack On League Commissioner

The Lonestar Times — September 24, 2026

What began as a routine inquiry into the surprising release of Max Dowman has erupted into one of the most extraordinary interviews in H-E-B Lonestar League history.

The Space Cadets chairman acknowledged that Dowman remains an exceptional long-term prospect, but argued that the structure of the Lonestar League makes carrying developmental players increasingly difficult.

“Dowman is a man for the future, yes. To be frank he just isn’t going to see the time in the field at this point in time.”

According to Space Cadets leadership, the problem isn’t Dowman. It’s the league.

“He needed to be cut loose because the commissioner of this league doesn’t value the youth on team rosters. There’s no value in holding onto a youth stud for years to come when they take up a place on the roster.”
“The minor slots are worthless, meaningless and a fugaze for all to see.”

Baleba Over Dowman

Space Cadets instead appear to be betting on youth with a clearer path toward immediate first-team minutes. The chairman pointed specifically toward Carlos Baleba, whom the club believes could soon earn significant playing time following the international break.

“Down With The Commish”

Asked whether supporters could expect progress toward an official club crest, the chairman did not answer the question. Instead, he launched an extraordinary attack on the league commissioner.

“THE COMMISSIONER IS A TYRANNICAL LUNATIC AND DOESNT LISTEN TO THE PEOPLE! DOWN WITH THE COMMISH.”

“Imma Win, Imma Steal, Imma Cheat”

“Imma win, imma steal, imma cheat! Imma do anything to make this team successful and fuck anything or anyone who stand is our way. It’s Mars or bust baby.”

Mars or bust. Three words that may now define the entire club. Whether the comments regarding “stealing” and “cheating” were merely colorful rhetoric or something the Commissioner’s Office intends to investigate remains unclear.

The Commissioner’s Office has yet to respond. Space Cadets aren't asking permission anymore. They’re going to Mars.

A New Era For The Lonestar League: New Look, New Home — And The First Trophy Is Almost Here

The Lonestar Times

The Lonestar League has a new look. And now, it has a new home.

League officials have officially unveiled the new H-E-B Lonestar League identity, marking the beginning of a more ambitious era for the competition. Alongside the refreshed league branding comes the launch of the league’s new official website.

What began as a fantasy competition between friends is increasingly developing its own history, rivalries and culture — and the league now has an identity built to match it.

The Five Star Shootout Reaches Its Final Week

The inaugural Five Star Shootout, sponsored by Best Buy, reaches its conclusion next week in Gameweek 6.

Each week, clubs are ranked by their total fantasy score. First place earns 25 Shootout points, with the remaining positions receiving 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4 and 2 points.

The highest individual single-week fantasy score across the entire competition earns a five-point bonus. After Gameweek 6, the points from all five rounds will be added together. The club sitting at the top wins the Five Star Shootout Cup.

More Than A Trophy

Thanks to Best Buy's sponsorship, the inaugural champion will also receive a $25 Best Buy digital gift card. But someone is also about to become the first trophy winner in H-E-B Lonestar League history.

A new league identity. A new sponsor. A new website. A dedicated home for the table, clubs, results and the increasingly ridiculous collection of stories emerging every week.

Gameweek 6. The Five Star Shootout. The first trophy in Lonestar League history is on the line.
